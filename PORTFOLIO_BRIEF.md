## 4. AI: the headline theme

AI must be prominent. Two strands: **AI products he designed or built**, and **AI in his own workflow**. Both are covered by real material below.

### 4a. AI products (LIQUiDITY)
1. **AI assistant** in the 2025 platform: contextual help.
2. **AI chat tool**, similar to Claude desktop: skills, projects, persona-based actionable home screens.
3. **Design-system-aware generative UI:** AI-generated UI matches the branded UI when data comes from the system of record.
4. **MCP app UIs:** built directly with Claude, with read, write and upload actions against real data.
5. **Designing against complacency:** see 4c.

### 4b. AI in his workflow (his process, lightly edited)
- **Tools:** mostly Claude and Claude Code, plus Figma AI.
- **MCP UI workflow:** the AI engineering team onboards "playbooks" per user task (e.g. structuring a loan), whose operations fetch from and write back to the system of record. Chetan uses Claude to list all operations available in a playbook, connect to the design system, and create the workflow in Figma via the Figma MCP (needed only for user review). Once approved, he connects Claude to Figma again to produce a **working MCP UI with real data coming in and writing back**. They considered removing the Figma step but kept it as a record for checking visual design and layout before building.
- **Throughput (real, his own estimate):** "If I designed these MCP UIs in Figma manually (they are pretty complex), I could have prepared at most 2–3 UIs if I fully focused. With this approach I created **12 different UIs**, with read, write and upload action screens."
- **Design system sync:** the Storybook and the Figma design system are connected centrally to Claude. He asks Claude to fetch specs from Figma and create new React components. When a component changes in Storybook (e.g. a new danger variant of a button), he uses Claude to add that variant to the Figma library so the team can use it in mocks. "The never-ending delta between the component library in Storybook and the Figma component system has become very thin."
- **Engineering guardrail:** even when he pushes components to Storybook directly, every PR is reviewed by the front-end engineering team.

### 4c. Where he does not trust AI (judgement: important for principal level)
- On tight timelines, building many screens and components with AI creates a "black box" feeling: you don't know if the AI deviated by introducing colours outside the foundation library or using components that haven't been built. **He reviews for this, and writes rules in project folders that the AI must follow.**
- He doesn't always trust AI with user flows. Even with PRDs and UX research notes as input, he checks thoroughly that the flow matches what was learnt from users and that the solution fits.
- **Designing against complacency in the product:** as users are pushed to rely on AI, many loans will eventually be generated by AI once financials, projections, facility details and use of proceeds are provided. "It is human to become complacent." He asked the Data Science and AI engineering teams to build against this, and to design **random check questions** that the AI chat asks investment managers about a fund-seeker's financial data. ## 6. Case study template (use for every case study)

Recruiters skim. Every case study opens with a summary block, then tells the story. Keep it to what mattered.

1. **Summary block**: project, company, year(s), duration, platform, his role, team, one-line outcome.
2. **Context**: the product, the business, who uses it (1 short paragraph).
3. **The problem**: what was broken, for whom, and what it cost.
4. **Users**: who they are, how expert, what they were trying to do.
5. **My role**: what *he* owned, decided or pushed for, separate from the team.
6. **Constraints**: legacy tech, regulation, deadlines, data limits.
7. **Process highlights**: only the steps that changed the outcome (research, audit, IA, flows, prototypes, testing).
8. **Key decisions and trade-offs**: including at least one where business or engineering won over a UX principle and how he flagged the caveat. This is his signature approach, so make it visible.
9. **The solution**: visuals first (before/after, flows, key screens), short captions.
10. **Outcome**: honest evidence only (see §2 Honesty rules). Shipped scope, dates, adoption, quotes, labelled estimates.
11. **Reflection**: what he'd do differently.
12. **NDA note** where relevant (blurred or recreated data).

Each case study is a copy of `case-studies/_template.html` (see "Adding a case study" in `CLAUDE.md`). Restyle the template to match the home page first.
